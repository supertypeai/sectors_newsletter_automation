# Caption (the post copy, not the slides)

*Runtime entry is `./writing.md` (section 4); open this file for the full treatment and worked examples.*

The caption is not an afterthought, it's a **second hook**. Instagram truncates a caption after roughly its first ~125 characters behind a "... more" tap, so the caption's first line is read exactly like a cover: most people never tap through the rest. Everything in `./viral-hooks.md` about the four hook killers (delay, confusion, irrelevance, disinterest) applies to that first line too, it's the same craft, a different unit of attention (`viral-hooks.md`'s own "repurposing note" already says this).

**The one rule that changes:** the caption's first line must **not restate the cover's headline verbatim**. If the cover already said it, repeating it in the caption is dead air, the caption's job is to deepen or re-angle, add the fact that didn't fit on a slide, or name the stake more directly ("this is you if you hold BBRI").

## Structure

1. **Hook line** (the part before the truncation): a re-angled or deepened version of the deck's thesis, never a copy of slide 1. Follow the same four-killer discipline as a cover.
2. **Body** (2-4 short sentences): expand the payoff in prose, the way you'd explain it out loud to a friend after they finished swiping. Plain words, real numbers, `./brand-voice.md` throughout, no new claims the deck doesn't already back.
3. **One real, specific, on-topic question** (optional but effective): tied directly to the deck's content, answerable in one line ("Do you hold BBRI? Does the payout cut change your read?"). This is different from banned engagement bait, see below, the test is whether the question is *about this piece's actual finding* or a generic prompt that would fit any post.
4. **3-5 relevant hashtags**, not a long tail: the ticker (`#BBRI`), the theme (`#IDXBanks`), `#IDX` or `#SectorsApp`. Hashtags are for categorization now, not reach, don't pad the count.

## Write the caption to be found, not just read

Instagram increasingly treats caption text as searchable content (a search for a topic can match plain words in a caption the way it matches a hashtag). Write the caption in plain English that naturally contains the ticker, the company name, and the topic words, this is already what `brand-voice.md` asks for anyway, it just means the caption earns search traffic as a side effect of being well-written, not from stuffing keywords.

## The good question vs. banned bait

`./anti-ai-writing.md` already bans generic engagement bait ("tag a friend," "read that again," "comment YES if..."). That ban stands. A **good** caption question is the opposite of bait: it's specific to this deck's finding and would be a non-sequitur on any other post.
- ❌ Bait (banned): "Tag someone who needs to see this!" / "Comment 🔥 if you agree!"
- ✅ On-topic (encouraged): "BBCA's ROE beats BBRI by 3.3 points, does the wider lending margin still make BBRI the better long-term hold for you?"

## Send it to someone, not just save it

A save is a private bookmark; a **send** (to a friend, to a DM, to a Story) is worth more to reach, and it needs a reason: give the reader someone specific to picture sending it to. The deck's own payoff slide (`./storytelling.md`'s "last dab") already built the insight, the caption's job is to make the hand-off explicit when it fits naturally: "send this to whoever still thinks BBRI is the safer bank."

## Worked example

```
Deck thesis: BBCA has a wider ROE despite a smaller lending margin than BBRI.

Caption:
BBCA earns more per rupiah of shareholder equity than BBRI, even with a
narrower spread on every loan. The difference is what each bank does with
its deposits, not what it charges to lend.

Full breakdown of the margin-vs-return gap in the carousel, cheaper deposits,
lower loan-loss provisioning, and where the two banks trade today.

Do you hold either? Does a wider margin change how you'd read the risk?

#BBCA #BBRI #IDXBanks
```

Notice the caption's first line doesn't repeat a cover headline like "A wider margin, a smaller return," it states the SAME finding from a different angle (efficiency, not margin), which is what makes it worth reading past the truncation even for someone who already saw slide 1.

## The checklist (every caption, before it ships)

- [ ] First line does NOT restate the cover headline verbatim, it deepens or re-angles?
- [ ] Runs through the four hook killers same as a cover (delay, confusion, irrelevance, disinterest)?
- [ ] Every claim already backed by a slide, no new number introduced only in the caption?
- [ ] If there's a question, is it specific to this deck's finding, not generic bait? (`anti-ai-writing.md`)
- [ ] 3-5 relevant hashtags, not a long tail?
- [ ] Plain, keyword-natural English, not stuffed?
- [ ] Passes `./anti-ai-writing.md` and matches `./brand-voice.md`?
