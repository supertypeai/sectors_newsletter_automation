# Storytelling

*Runtime entry is `./writing.md` (section 1); open this file for the full treatment and worked examples.*

The hook earns the first slide. **Storytelling earns every swipe after.** This is the craft of holding attention across an IDX carousel, so the reader who stopped on the cover swipes to the payoff and shares it.

The medium is a **carousel, not a spoken reel**. The story is told **across slides, one beat per slide**. There is no runtime; there is a slide budget. Six techniques: the first four do most of the work, the last two are delivery and handoff.

## When this fires

- **Always**, on any story-driven or opinion deck (an earnings turn, a valuation re-rate, an ownership twist, a smart-money flow story).
- **The Dance and Rhythm apply even to "facts about a ticker" decks**: any carousel with a through-line benefits from open loops and varied slide density.
- **Direction and Story Lens shine most on a deck with a take**: where there's a turn or an angle, not just a number.
- **Skip** only for a single-slide explainer or a pure reference table with no arc.

## Before you write: real beats only (NO-FABRICATION, NO-ADVICE)

These override every craft rule below. Get them right or the deck does not ship.

- **Never invent a number, price, or event.** Every beat is a real Sectors API field or a cited source. No estimates dressed as facts, no rounded-from-memory prices, no imagined "and the CEO said." If you don't have the beat, pull it or drop it. See `../sectors-api/data-quality.md`.
- **Describe, never advise.** No buy, no sell, no "this is cheap, load up," no price target framed as our call. We report what the data shows; the reader decides. See `./brand-voice.md`.
- Structure is this skill's job. The truth is the data's. A turn that needs a fake number to land is not a turn; find the real one.

## Three editorial laws (the eval taught us these)

- **Story first, data second.** Find the angle by *research* (web search for what is moving now) and write the thesis + beats *before* you fetch numbers. Numbers are evidence for a story you already have; they are not the story. Decks built from raw data outward read as dashboards, not posts. This assumes a subject is already in hand. When the user delegates the topic entirely (an open ask: "pick something," "what's worth posting"), SKILL.md step 1's cheap API discovery signals legitimately come before the web search, that's a narrower research step to surface a candidate subject, not a violation of story first: the thesis and beats still get written from research before any beat gets fetched for real. See SKILL.md step 1.
- **Every slide states a verdict.** The reader should never have to do the analysis. Don't put "EPS growth 4.9%" on a slide and move on, say "growth has nearly stopped, the slowest in five years." The number is the proof; the sentence is the point. A slide with no verdict is a slide with no reason to exist.
- **Omit openly, never curate silently.** If a data point is null, implausible, or off-thesis, drop it and re-angle around what is real, and say what you dropped. Cutting inconvenient years to make a trend look cleaner is bending the data to the story, which is the one thing we never do.
- **Recent is the story; history is the proof, never the headline.** The thesis has to center on something that actually happened or changed recently, a print, a filing, a rate move, a flow reversal, a rally or plunge in the last days/weeks/quarter, not a standing fact that's always been true. A 5-year streak, a multi-year ratio, a long-run comparison is real, useful evidence that the recent thing is genuine or unusual, it belongs in the beats, but it can't be the lead. "TLKM has paid a dividend every year since 2020" is a reference fact; "TLKM just raised its payout past 100% even as profit fell" is a post. If research turns up nothing that changed recently, that's a signal to pick a different subject, not to lead with the historical pattern instead, see SKILL.md step 1's "why now" requirement, this is the same discipline stated as a story-craft law, not just a research step.

**Why the benchmarking discipline matters for engagement, not just accuracy:** of Jonah Berger's six STEPPS sharing triggers (Social Currency, Triggers, Emotion, Public, Practical Value, Stories), `brand-voice.md`'s no-hype/no-drama rules deliberately forgo Emotion and Social Currency in exchange for credibility. That means **Practical Value**, real, useful, benchmarked information, has to carry almost the whole reason someone saves or sends a piece. This is exactly what "every beat needs a benchmark" (SKILL.md step 2) and "every slide states a verdict" above already build, the rules aren't just about rigor, they're this account's actual engagement lever. Don't compensate for a thin beat by reaching for drama or hype instead, that trades away the one lever this brand voice has left.

## 1. The Dance: context vs. conflict (the engine)

Great stories alternate **context** (here is what's happening) and **conflict** (but here's the complication). Conflict opens a loop in the brain; context closes it, then opens the next. On a carousel that dance lives in the **transitions between slides**.

**The tactical rule (from the South Park writers):** every slide-to-slide transition is a **BUT** or a **THEREFORE**, never "and then another stat." "And then" piles number on number with no tension and the reader swipes away. "But/therefore" keeps a loop open across the swipe.

**Weak (and-then stat piling, one boring slide after another):**
> BBRI posted record profit. And revenue grew. And the dividend rose. And ROE is high. And foreigners sold. And the stock fell.

**Strong (but/therefore loops across slides):**
> *S2:* BBRI just posted its highest net profit ever. *S3:* **THEREFORE** you'd expect the stock to rip. *S4:* **BUT** foreign investors sold a net Rp 1.4T into the print. *S5:* **THEREFORE** the story isn't the earnings beat... *S6 (payoff):* it's who's quietly walking out the door.

Outline the deck as a column of beats and force a "but" or "therefore" between each. If "and then" fits the gap, that slide is filler. Cut it or sharpen it into a real turn.

## 2. Direction: write the payoff slide first

The best place to start a carousel is the **end**. Write the **payoff slide** (the last content slide, the "so what") before anything else, then work backward to the cover hook.

**Make the payoff the "last dab":** so memorable that if someone read only that one slide, they'd send it. It is also the slide that justifies the cover's promise, so write the cover hook to set up exactly this payoff.

**The outro is fixed.** The renderer auto-appends a branded outro slide (`Like this post?` + search-pill CTA). Do **not** write outro copy; your last authored slide is the payoff, not a sign-off. See `../house-style.md` §8.

**Tactic:** write the payoff slide and a working cover line first. Leave the middle empty. Fill it with but/therefore beats that earn the payoff.

- Weak last content slide: "...and that's the quarter. Follow for more!"
- Last dab payoff: "The earnings were never the story. The exit was."

## 3. Story Lens: the non-obvious angle

A ticker isn't an angle; everyone covers BBCA earnings. The **lens** is your prism on it, the same data bent differently. The uncommon lens is how a deck becomes the one people screenshot instead of one more numbers post.

List the obvious lenses first, then push past them. The moves, in IDX terms:

- **Invert the villain.** The obvious villain (the price drop) usually isn't the real one. The real story isn't that GOTO fell; it's *who* sold the share register on the way down.
- **Find the asset in the failure.** The "disappointing" segment is often the fastest grower; the loss-maker is buying market share. The thing that looks like weakness is the product.
- **Jump to the second-order effect.** Don't cover the rate cut. Cover what it quietly re-rates: the property names that borrow to build, not just the banks.
- **Switch the POV.** Tell TLKM from the **foreign investor's seat**, not local retail's. The flows read completely differently from there.
- **Flip the cost/benefit.** The buyback everyone cheered was funded out of the dividend they'll now miss.

Pick the lens **before** writing. It changes every slide after it.

## 4. Rhythm: vary the copy and vary the slides

Two rhythms. **Within a slide,** vary sentence length. Same-length lines in a row drone. Short. Then medium. Then, once in a while, a longer line that builds and rolls to a close.

> It hit a record.
> Foreign funds sold into it for six straight sessions, trimming a position they'd held for years.
> The stock fell.

Short. Long. Short. That's the music. Write each line on its own row and look down the left edge: a **jagged edge** means lengths vary; a flat edge means it drones, so break some up and run some together.

**Across the deck,** vary slide density too. A big-number slide (one `stat hero`), then a chart slide (`quarterlyTrend`), then a one-line `insight`. A run of three identical dense charts in a row is the visual version of monotone. Let the deck breathe (see `../house-style.md` §9).

## 5. Tone: defer to brand-voice

Write to **one smart, time-poor friend** who follows IDX, not "an audience." Confident, plain, specific, no performing and no hype. This is a **brand voice, not a person**, so the target lives in `./brand-voice.md`, not in any creator's voice file. Where this skill says "match the voice," read that file. If a slide sounds like a sell-side report or a finfluencer, rewrite it as something you'd actually text a friend.

## 6. The Hook: handoff to viral-hooks

The hook is last here on purpose: it's critical, but it's a **separate skill**. Use `./viral-hooks.md` to write and stop-test the **cover (slide 1)**. Two things storytelling needs from it: the cover line must be **punchy and indicative of the plot** (not "wait for slide 5"), and it must promise exactly the payoff you wrote in §2. Don't duplicate hook mechanics here.

**Frame: you/they, not I.** This is a brand account telling a *company and market* story, so default to a **you/they** frame about the ticker and the market ("BBRI posted... foreign investors sold..."). The personal I-story from the source genre does **not** apply; there is no founder on this account. Relevance comes from the reader **recognizing a market they follow**, not from a creator's biography. If a draft slips into "I think the stock," cut it back to what the data shows.

**Slide budget (the one hard constraint):** a carousel is **3–10 slides**: cover + 3–8 content slides + the fixed outro. **One beat per content slide.** Never cram two turns onto one slide; if a slide makes two points, it's two slides. Deck length follows the story's depth, not a fixed count. Cut beats before you cut the lens or the payoff.

## Beat → block: pick the visual that carries the turn

Each beat is a slide, and its **lead block** should *be* the turn, not decorate it. Match the beat to the most specific block (see `../house-style.md` §7):

- earnings turn → `quarterlyTrend` · valuation point → `rangeBar`, or a `multiline` of the ratio vs its peer average (`valuation.historical_valuation`) · ownership twist → `ownership`
- smart-money beat → a `bar` chart of daily `foreign-flow` net inflow (positive/negative days color themselves) or `ranking` for top brokers · price move → `priceSnapshot` / `rangeBar` · payoff "so what" → `insight` card

Pick the block that makes the beat unarguable. A number with no source does not go on a slide.

## Writing mode (drafting a deck)

1. **Beats**: pull the real, sourced beats first (no-fabrication rule). No beats, no deck.
2. **Lens**: pick the unique angle before writing a line (§3).
3. **Direction**: write the payoff slide and a working cover line; leave the middle empty (§2).
4. **Dance**: fill the middle with but/therefore beats, one per slide; kill every "and then" (§1).
5. **Blocks**: assign each beat its lead block (beat → block map above).
6. **Rhythm**: one line per row inside each slide; check the jagged edge; vary slide density (§4).
7. **Tone**: read it as if texting one friend; rewrite anything that sounds like a report (§5, `./brand-voice.md`).
8. **Hook**: finalize the cover via `./viral-hooks.md` (§6), replacing the step-3 working line.
9. **Budget**: trim to 3–8 content slides; one beat per slide; cut beats, keep the lens and payoff.

## Audit mode (checking a draft)

Score and flag with the specific offending slide:
- **Recency:** does the lead sentence name something that happened/changed recently, or is it a standing historical fact with no fresh trigger? → flag, find the recent hook or don't run the piece.
- **Dance:** count transitions that are "and then another stat" with no tension → flag each; rewrite as but/therefore.
- **Direction:** is the last content slide a shareable payoff, or does it trail off / pre-empt the fixed outro? → flag.
- **Lens:** is the angle the obvious one anyone would take? → flag, propose a sharper lens.
- **Blocks:** does each slide's lead block carry its beat, or is it generic? → flag, name the better block.
- **Rhythm:** monotone copy in a slide, or a run of identical-density slides? → flag the flat stretch.
- **Tone & truth:** any slide that sounds like a report, gives advice, or carries an unsourced number? → flag, fix.

## The checklist (every carousel)

- [ ] Real, sourced beats only: zero invented numbers, prices, or events?
- [ ] The lead is something that happened/changed recently, not a standing historical fact (history backs it up, it isn't the headline)?
- [ ] No advice: describe, never prescribe?
- [ ] A unique lens, not the obvious angle?
- [ ] Payoff slide written first; it's a shareable "so what," not a sign-off (outro is auto-appended)?
- [ ] But/therefore between every slide: zero "and then"?
- [ ] One beat per slide; 3–8 content slides inside budget?
- [ ] Each beat on the most specific block (earnings→quarterlyTrend, etc.)?
- [ ] Jagged edge inside slides, varied density across the deck?
- [ ] Sounds like one friend talking, in the `./brand-voice.md` voice?
- [ ] Cover handled by `./viral-hooks.md` (punchy, plot-indicative, promises the payoff)?

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | Why it fails |
|---|---|
| "And then... and then..." stat-stacked slides | No tension, no open loops; the reader swipes away mid-deck |
| Generic arc ("company struggled, then did well") | Everyone's deck; the lens is what makes it yours |
| Trailing-off last slide / "follow for more" | Wastes the payoff, and the branded outro already closes the deck |
| Two beats crammed on one slide | Breaks one-beat-per-slide; the turn gets buried |
| Generic block for a specific beat (a plain `stat` for an earnings turn) | The visual doesn't carry the story; use `quarterlyTrend` etc. |
| Flat rhythm (same-length lines, or a run of identical dense charts) | Drones; pushes the reader to the next post |
| An I-frame opinion or any advice | Off-brand; it's a market story, and we describe, never prescribe |
| Any unsourced or invented number | Fabrication breaks the exact trust the deck is built to earn |
