# Narrative approach: "storytelling" vs "teaser"

The third axis, alongside theme (`references/themes.md`) and length (`references/length.md`).
This one isn't a `storyboard.json` field — it's a writing stance that shapes which beats you
pick in step 2 and how the final scene closes in step 6. Decide it, then write to it; there is
no lint check for it because it's a copy discipline, not a structural one.

## "storytelling"

The piece resolves. `cover` hooks, proof scenes build the case (history, structure, numbers,
a fun fact), and `takeaway` states the finding plainly — the viewer knows the whole shape of
the story by the last frame. This is the default for a company deep-dive: history timeline,
structure, and a fun fact are all things a viewer learns and the piece confirms, not things
left hanging.

Use for: an earnings/dividend story with a clear verdict, an ownership structure reveal, a
"here's what this company actually is" deep-dive, most `"long"` company pieces.

`takeaway` copy pattern: a single plain finding sentence, same as `scenes.md` describes —
"A high yield can be a warning, not a reward." Never a cliffhanger, never advice-framed.

## "teaser"

The piece deliberately withholds the resolution. `cover` still hooks, proof scenes still use
real cited numbers, but the sequence stops short of the full answer — it raises a specific,
answerable question ("So who actually controls the dividend decision?", "But the float rule
changes in 30 days.") and the final scene names that open question instead of resolving it,
then points at where the answer lives: the outro `tagline` (the ticker's `sectors.app/idx/...`
page), or an explicit follow-up mention ("Part 2 next.") if a follow-up piece is already
planned. This is a suspense device, not a bait-and-switch — every number shown must still be
real; only the LAST piece of the puzzle is withheld, never a fabricated cliffhanger.

Use for: building anticipation ahead of a second post, driving traffic to Sectors' own
analytics for the reader to look up the rest, a market-event story whose full resolution is
still unfolding (numbers not final yet) where honestly saying "still developing" beats forcing
a premature verdict.

`takeaway` copy pattern (still hard-rule-2 compliant, still no fabricated stakes): name the
open question in plain words, point to where it resolves. "The payout ratio keeps climbing.
What happens when profit doesn't." reads as a teaser finding, not a prescription — it does not
say what SHOULD happen, only that the question is open.

## What doesn't change between the two

Hard rules 1-3 (real numbers, no advice, plausibility-checked) apply identically to both —
"teaser" is a structural choice about WHEN information is revealed, never a license to imply
something unverified. Cover, proof-scene, and body-copy discipline (`scenes.md`,
`writing/writing.md`) are unchanged; only the closing beat's job differs.

## Picking between them

If genuinely unsure, ask which the user wants — same rule as theme selection in
`themes.md`. A quick signal: if the user says "make people curious", "want them to check
sectors.app for the rest", or "tease this before a bigger post", that's teaser; "explain",
"deep dive", "tell the full story", or "history of" is storytelling.
