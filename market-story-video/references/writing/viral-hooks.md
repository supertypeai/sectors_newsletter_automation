# Viral Hooks (Carousel Cover)

*Runtime entry is `./writing.md` (section 2); open this file for the full treatment and worked examples.*

On Instagram a carousel lives or dies on **slide 1**. The cover IS the hook. It has exactly ONE job: help the right swiper decide to swipe to slide 2. To do that it must deliver two things, and only two:

1. **Topic clarity:** at a glance, the swiper knows exactly which company and which story this is. The gold **kicker** (`IDX · EARNINGS`) does half this work, which frees the headline to spend its words on curiosity.
2. **On-target curiosity:** the swiper believes this is for THEM, and an open question pulls them forward. On finance covers that question is almost always a **gap**: what they assume vs. what the data actually shows.

A cover that's clever but unclear fails. A cover that's clear but flat fails. Both, or it isn't done.

**The cover is three parts** (see `../house-style.md` §8): a gold **kicker**, a **display headline with one gradient emphasis word** (the hook line renders base-colour with only the verdict word/number in gradient, ≤ ~9 words, 1–3 short lines), and **one** support (a subhead OR a single stat, never both). Throughout this skill, example hooks are shown as a **kicker + headline** pair, because that is the unit the swiper actually reads.

**A realistic word count and the type ramp, reconciled:** a real financial headline that hits ~6-9 words almost always runs past the 22-character threshold in `../house-style.md`'s type ramp, so expect the 84px display tier most of the time, that's the normal case, not a failure. The 104px tier is for a genuinely short punch headline, 2-4 words ("Profit grew. Losses too."), not the default target to aim for.

**Always match the brand voice.** Before writing covers, read `./brand-voice.md`. It is the voice every carousel matches; there is no personal voice file, so the brand voice IS the target. The patterns below are the mechanics; brand-voice.md is the sound: plain, specific, numerate, never hype, never advice.

**If you can't check the voice** (mid-task, file not loaded): proceed on the mechanics and say so in the output (`"voice unverified, not checked against brand-voice.md"`). Never block, never silently pretend the voice check happened.

**Two hard rules the hook can never break** (brand-voice.md non-negotiables):
- **No fabrication.** Never promise a number or a claim the deck can't deliver. If the headline says "highest ROE in five years," a slide must show it from a real Sectors field. A bait cover is the fastest way to lose trust. See `../sectors-api/data-quality.md`.
- **No advice.** The hook describes, it never prescribes. No "buy," "sell," "load up," "this is cheap." Report what the data shows; the reader decides.

## The Four Hook Killers

Every weak cover fails in one of exactly four ways. Diagnose which one, apply the fix.

### 1. DELAY: the topic arrives too late
Every word before the topic costs you swipers. On a cover the deadline is the **thumb-stop**: the headline is read in a single glance, so any throat-clearing is fatal.
- ❌ `IDX · BANKING` / "Okay so I have been digging into the banks and found something kind of wild."
- ✅ `IDX · BANKING` / "One bank earns more per rupiah than any rival."

**Fix:** Delete everything before the topic. No greeting, no "let me explain." Let the kicker carry the category so the headline can open straight on the subject.

### 2. CONFUSION: the swiper has to re-read
Nested clauses, abstract words, jargon. If the headline takes two passes to parse, the swipe is already gone. The ≤ ~9 word limit exists for exactly this.
- ❌ "The persistence of BBCA's valuation premium reflects a structural return-on-equity differential versus peers."
- ✅ "BBCA trades at a premium. One number says why."

**Fix:** Fewer words. Plain words. One idea per line. Explain any ratio in a clause the first time it appears (brand-voice.md).

### 3. IRRELEVANCE: clear, but not for ME
The swiper understands the topic but can't see themselves in it. The #1 cause: framing it as the creator's interest, not the reader's stake.
- ❌ "I've tracked IDX foreign flows for years." (Swiper: do I care about your tracking? Out.)
- ✅ "If you hold BBRI, here's who's been quietly selling it." (Aimed at them; they can't opt out.)

**Fix:** Frame around what the swiper **holds, watches, or assumes**. Speak to the BBRI holder, to the person who saw GOTO's drop, to the one who thinks the biggest bank is automatically the safest bet.

### 4. DISINTEREST: clear, relevant, still skippable
Clarity and relevance are there, but no question is left open. The cure is **contrast**: the gap between what the swiper believes (A) and what the data shows (B) IS the curiosity. No gap, no pull. This is the single strongest lever for finance covers, because the data so often contradicts the assumption.
- **Stated contrast** (blunt, impossible to miss): "Everyone watched the price. The real story was who was buying."
- **Implied contrast** (savvier, only B stated, A left implied): "Indonesia's most valuable company is also its most boring."

**Fix:** Name the A (the common belief: bigger = riskier, a falling price = bad news, a high P/E = overpriced) and put the data's B against it. The bigger the believable distance, the stronger the cover.

## What "the hook" is on a carousel

The cover is the whole hook, read top to bottom:
- **Kicker** (gold mono eyebrow, e.g. `IDX · DIVIDENDS`): the topic category. Earns half the topic clarity for free.
- **Gradient headline** (≤ ~9 words, 1–3 short lines): the contrast / curiosity. This is the line you sweat.
- **One support** (a subhead line OR one stat): a single concrete proof point, never a stack. The optional 30-day spark backdrop is atmosphere, not the support.

A cover is a hook, not a meal. Don't crowd it (`../house-style.md` §8).

**Repurposing note:** the four killers are universal. If the same story also goes out as a reel caption, an X post, or a newsletter subject line, the craft transfers unchanged. Only the unit of attention moves (first line, first tweet, subject). The carousel cover is the home format; everything else is a port.

## Hook shape: the headline can be 1–3 lines

The best covers do clarity + contrast in one line. But the headline is allowed to run 1–3 short lines:
- Line 1: topic / subject
- Lines 2–3: the contrast or the open question

Don't sacrifice clarity to force a one-liner. And don't lean on the support line to make a thin headline make sense; the headline should stand mostly on its own.

## Banned openers
These always get flagged. If real substance follows the wrapper, only the wrapper dies (delete it, pure DELAY cost). If the substance is missing, the whole hook dies:
- "Let me explain" / "let's talk about": throat-clearing (DELAY)
- "A breakdown:" / "Thread:" / "Story time:" with no claim in the same breath: empty wrapper (DELAY)
- "The stock nobody's watching" / "nobody talks about this" without naming it: vague + finfluencer cliché (CONFUSION)
- "[Company] released Q3 earnings": a news report, no angle, no stake for the reader (IRRELEVANCE)
- "The shift happening in Indonesian banking…": philosophical fog (all four killers at once)
- A CTA as the opener ("Follow for more IDX breakdowns"): CTAs close, they never open
- "You won't believe this chart" / "wait till you see this": zero topic clarity (DELAY)
- **Advice or hype as a hook** ("Buy BBCA now," "this blue chip will 10x," "your sign to load up"): breaks the no-advice and no-hype rules in brand-voice.md. Flag and rewrite.

## Writing mode
1. **Know the angle and the data behind it.** Pick the story (an earnings surprise, a dividend streak, a foreign-selling shift, a valuation gap, a drawdown) and confirm the deck can actually back the claim. No fabrication.
2. **Absorb the voice** from `./brand-voice.md` (cadence, vocabulary, the off-brand vs. on-brand table). This replaces "read your top posts."
3. **Vary the angle, not the words.** Build each variant around a different lens on the story (the number, the contrast, the who, the why-now) and lead with a concrete specific by default (a real figure, a named company, an odd number).
4. Run every headline through the four-killer checklist below, plus the deliverable and advice checks.
5. **Deliver 3 cover variants** by default, each a different angle, each as a **kicker + headline** pair, each deliverable by the deck. Label the angle on each so the choice is real, not three rewordings.

## Audit mode
Audit the cover as a unit. Score each killer **pass** or **flag with the exact offending words quoted**, then add the deliverable, advice, and voice checks. Then rewrite.

```
HOOK AUDIT (cover)
  kicker:   "IDX · BANKING"
  headline: "This boring bank quietly became the most profitable, let me explain"
  Delay:       FLAG, "let me explain" postpones the payoff; the kicker already set the topic
  Confusion:   pass
  Irrelevance: pass, speaks to anyone watching IDX banks
  Disinterest: pass, "boring" vs. "most profitable" is a real A-vs-B gap
  Deliverable: FLAG, is "most profitable" shown on a slide? (no fabrication; see ../sectors-api/data-quality.md)
  Advice:      pass, describes, doesn't say buy/sell
  Voice:       FLAG vs. ./brand-voice.md, drop "let me explain," tighten to plain declaratives
  Rewrite:
    kicker:   "IDX · BANKING"
    headline: "Indonesia's most boring bank is also its most profitable."
```

Never flag without quoting the words that triggered it. Never rewrite without checking `./brand-voice.md` (say "voice unverified" if you can't).

## The checklist (every cover, before it ships)
- [ ] Topic clear at the thumb-stop, kicker + headline together? (no DELAY)
- [ ] Headline ≤ ~9 words, understood on one pass? (no CONFUSION)
- [ ] Aimed at someone who follows IDX, around what they hold/watch/assume? (no IRRELEVANCE)
- [ ] An A-vs-B gap that opens a real question? (no DISINTEREST)
- [ ] Every claim backed by a real slide and field, no number invented, no buy/sell? (deliverable; brand-voice.md + data-quality.md)
- [ ] Sounds like `./brand-voice.md`, not a finfluencer and not a template? (voice check)

All six pass → the cover is working.

## Common mistakes
| Mistake | Why it fails |
|---|---|
| Three variants that are one idea reworded | No real choice. Vary the angle (earnings vs. flows vs. valuation), not the synonyms |
| Cover promises a number the deck can't show | Readers punish bait; trust dies on slide 2, and it breaks the no-fabrication rule |
| Shock over believability | Contrast only works inside the reader's belief range. "This stock will 10x" is dead on arrival and off-brand |
| Advice as a hook | "Buy BBCA now" breaks the no-advice rule. Describe the gap, don't prescribe the trade |
| Headline that needs the support line to make sense | The headline should stand mostly on its own; the support is proof, not a crutch |
| A cover for everyone | "Every Indonesian investor must read this" stops no one. Name the swiper (the BBRI holder, the one who saw the drop) |
