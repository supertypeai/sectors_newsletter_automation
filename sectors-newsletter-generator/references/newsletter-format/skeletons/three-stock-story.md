# Section skeleton: three-stock-story

One issue type's section skeleton, split out of `references/newsletter-format.md`. That file still owns every cross-type convention (header block, ticker mentions, UTM, color, prose style, number formatting, sources appendix, disclaimer, length). Read this file only for the type you are actually writing.

### Three-stock story

**Title.** A thematic headline naming the pattern the three picks share, written for a
reader who does not yet know the tickers ("How Indonesia's Tycoons Are Playing Their
Stocks"). Not a list of the three events, not a comma-chain of teasers.

**Subtitle.** One or two sentences saying why this pattern matters to a retail
subscriber — what is at stake for someone holding these names.

1. **Framing, two short paragraphs.** First paragraph previews all three stories in one
   sweep, plain language, no citations, no numbers beyond the one figure that carries
   each story. Second is a one-line bridge ("Here is a look at what happened"). If the
   three picks genuinely share no thread, say so here plainly instead of forcing one.
2. Three **per-stock sections**. Heading is a narrative label plus company plus ticker:
   `The Unexplained Rally of Intermedia Capital (MDIA)`,
   `The Strategic Trim of Impack Pratama Industri (IMPC)`. Never a bare company name.
   Each section has exactly **two** sub-parts:
   - *The Story & People* — two paragraphs. First: founding, control, one genuine fun
     fact, and who steers it now (founders, holding vehicles, key executives), history
     and people woven together rather than split into separate lists. Second: the
     current news hook, the "why this stock, why now."
   - *The Finances* — one paragraph of prose, not a field dump. Revenue and earnings
     trajectory, the one ratio that matters, and the forward view folded into the same
     paragraph as **attributed** statement (management guidance, disclosed strategy,
     named analyst). No separate outlook sub-head.
3. **Closing section with its own heading** — a short thematic title, then a paragraph
   that reads the three picks as points on one spectrum. This is synthesis, not a
   restatement of the three sections.
4. Comparison **table** after the closing paragraph, same metrics across all three.
5. **Sources** list + Appendix + disclaimer footer.
