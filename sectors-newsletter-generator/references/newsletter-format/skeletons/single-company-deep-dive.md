# Section skeleton: single-company-deep-dive

One issue type's section skeleton, split out of `references/newsletter-format.md`. That file still owns every cross-type convention (header block, ticker mentions, UTM, color, prose style, number formatting, sources appendix, disclaimer, length). Read this file only for the type you are actually writing.

### Single company deep dive

**Three body sections, not five.** The trigger, the argued read, and what to watch. Do not
give the one-line verdict its own standalone section, and do not split the numbers and the
valuation into separate sections, the valuation *is* part of the read and belongs in the
same block as the trend it reinterprets. Headline and every H2 in Title Case.

1. **Headline** — a declarative news sentence, Title Case, naming the company or ticker and
   the tension: event on the left, the thing it sits against on the right.
   `DSSA Releases Buyback Shares as Rally Outruns Earnings`. Tighter is better, cut
   possessives and qualifiers ("as Rally Outruns Earnings", not "as its rally outruns
   profit"). No question, no colon-subtitle.
2. **Standfirst** — three short declarative sentences, no heading, no inline citations, no
   comma-chained subordinate clauses. Sentence one, what the company did, with the size.
   Sentence two, what it has already caused. Sentence three, the counterweight, opened with
   a concessive ("Despite the pressure, the stock sits +131% above its 90-day low").
3. **The trigger and its mechanism** — H2 naming the market condition, not the event
   ("Supply Shock Meets Low Float"). Two paragraphs. First, how the trigger works: the rule
   or filing compelling it, the size in shares and as a percent of issued capital and of the
   treasury pile, and the party executing it. Second, the market print it left: negotiated
   volume, average price, the discount or premium to the regular close, net foreign flow that
   session, intraday low against the close.
   Open on the mechanism, not on the shareholder register. A float or ownership breakdown
   enters only as the one figure the argument needs ("only about a fifth of the stock trades
   freely"), never as a full holder-by-holder list.
4. **The argued read** — H2 stating the interpretation as a claim
   ("The Market Is Pricing the Turnaround Before It Arrives"). One block, in this order:
   - The move measured off a stated low, set against the multi-year fundamental trend that
     contradicts it, carried in prose: net profit across the last four fiscal years with the
     total percentage decline, ROE year by year, EPS year by year.
   - Valuation in the same breath: the name's own historical multiples before the run, then
     where the rally has pushed it relative to those and to the current sector peer average.
     Spell out a ratio once with its abbreviation, "price-to-earnings (P/E)", and use the
     abbreviation after.
   - The period table (latest quarter plus the last three fiscal years, revenue, net profit,
     YoY-change column), with any comparability break as a one-sentence footnote directly
     under it. Write the footnote as a plain statement of fact, never as an aside addressed
     to the reader about the chart.
   - **The counterargument, always, as its own paragraph**, opened by naming it as such
     ("The counterargument is that..."). Give it real data with the same specificity as the
     bear case: the segment growing and by how much, the facility and its capacity and guided
     start quarter, the pipeline under development. An issue that only stacks the bear case
     is a disguised sell call.
   - Close the block by putting the unresolved question to the reader in one sentence
     ("This creates the central investment debate: is the market simply paying too much for
     X, or correctly anticipating Y?"). State the question. Do not answer it
     (`../../compliance.md`).
5. **What to Watch Next** — three or four bullets, each strictly forward-looking, each with a
   threshold, quarter, or counterparty attached, and each closing on why it matters ("this
   could determine the short-term price ceiling"). Not a prose paragraph. Never a place for
   backward-looking trivia such as last month's institutional buyers and sellers, cut that
   entirely rather than parking it here.
6. **Primary CTA** — imperative and short, `Read DSSA company report`. Then the Upcoming
   events block if one applies, **Sources**, the endpoint Appendix, and the disclaimer footer
   (`../../newsletter-format.md`).

**Citation density.** Cite in the body only where a specific claim needs a named outside
source, roughly once per paragraph at most, and never twice in one sentence. Trailing
`(sectors.app)` after every figure is the single loudest tell of an unedited draft: the
Appendix and the standing "figures are from sectors.app" disclaimer already carry API
provenance, so figures pulled from the API need no inline tag at all.

**What this type is not.** No founder history, no "fun fact", no potted biography of the
controlling family unless the ownership move is itself the trigger.
