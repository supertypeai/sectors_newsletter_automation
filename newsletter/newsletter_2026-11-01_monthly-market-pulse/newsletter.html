IDX lost a sixth of its value in a month while foreign brokers sold

IDX total market cap closed the 30 days to 29 October at IDR 8,963.49T, down 16.66% from IDR 10,754.86T on 29 September across 23 trading days. The window peaked at IDR 10,892.24T on 6 October, then lost 10.89% in the single session of 16 October, the day MSCI opened its consultation on Indonesia's market status. The low came on 19 October at IDR 8,840.65T, 18.84% under the peak. A rebound to IDR 9,579.69T on 23 October gave back 6.43% by month-end, leaving the final close 1.39% above the low.

The Read

Foreign-flagged brokers sold a net IDR 16.97T over the window and domestic-flagged brokers bought IDR 17.04T, so the month's drawdown had one identifiable seller and one identifiable absorber. Three houses, ZP, AK and KZ, carry most of the foreign selling, and the index low arrived three days after the MSCI consultation opened. If that read is right, the next foreign-flag broker totals decide whether the low holds.

Macro Backdrop

MSCI opens a reclassification consultation (16 October). MSCI proposed moving Indonesia from Emerging Market to Standalone Market status, accepting feedback until 30 April 2027 and committing to a decision on or before 30 June 2027. The Jakarta Composite fell 11.57% to 5,185.10 that Friday after a 30-minute trading halt. The mechanism would be index-linked funds reducing exposure ahead of a possible change, which would show up first in the foreign houses ZP, AK and KZ, consistent with this month's broker table. The broker data cannot confirm that link.

Exchange rule changes (19 to 20 October, effective 2 November). From 19 October the exchange cut the daily auto-reject-bottom limit from 15% to 7% through 31 December, and 351 stocks hit it on the first day. IDX also confirmed the removal of about 80 high shareholding concentration stocks from the Jakarta Composite calculation, effective 2 November. The floor limit applies to every name in this issue's loser table, including FORU, VKTR, CUAN, PTRO and MLPT, and it caps how much of a decline can print in one session.

Rupiah, rates and oil (21 to 29 October). Bank Indonesia held its policy rate at 5.75% on 21 October and raised the deposit facility rate to 5% after 100 basis points of hikes in May and June. The rupiah then set a record low of IDR 18,183 per US dollar on 28 October, the 10-year government bond yield moved above 7.5%, and the 27 October bond auction raised 52.1% less than the one before it. The Fed held its rate with a hawkish tone and Brent traded near 114 US dollars a barrel on 29 October. Coal producers earn in US dollars, so a weaker rupiah and a high energy price would lift translated revenue at BUMI and DEWA; nothing in this issue's data confirms that effect.

The Other Side
The bull read
  • Domestic houses matched the foreign selling in full, so the market has a buyer base that exists regardless of how the MSCI consultation ends.
  • The consultation is a proposal with a long comment window, and the exchange has already moved on the governance topics MSCI's document asks about, through index composition and trading-limit changes.
  • Reported earnings at the volume leader kept arriving through the sell-off, with a buyback programme running alongside, which keeps the fundamental case separate from the price path.
The bear read
  • Foreign selling sits in a handful of houses, and a concentrated exit can resume as quickly as it paused, particularly with the currency at record lows giving foreign holders a second reason to reduce.
  • The domestic bid is finite. Weak demand at the government bond auction shows domestic capital being stretched across asset classes at the same time as it absorbs equity supply.
  • Tighter daily floor limits spread forced selling across more sessions, which stretches price discovery in the loser table over a longer stretch.
  • Index fixes change which names move the benchmark, while the governance questions that triggered the consultation stay open until MSCI decides.

What would settle it. The pattern in the 17 to 18 November Bank Indonesia decision and the foreign-flag broker totals for November decide between the two reads: continued net foreign selling after the rate decision supports the bear read, and a flip to net foreign buying supports the bull read.
