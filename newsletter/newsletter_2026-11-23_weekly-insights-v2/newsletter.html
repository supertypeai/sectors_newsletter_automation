What the Data Unearthed
LQ45 fell 2.66% and total market cap fell 3.56%, with mining names leading the drop
• The broader market gave back all of last week's gain while LQ45 kept part of it: total market cap closed 0.21% below its 6 November level, and LQ45 stayed 3.37% above it.
• Mining names set the pace: CUAN, INDY, MDKA, DEWA and AMMN make up five of the eight weakest LQ45 constituents, and the week opened with President Prabowo Subianto calling for the state to take at least 70% of mining profits.
• Earnings did not hold the prices up, since INDY reported sharply higher nine-month profit and fell -22.40%, and MDKA swung to a nine-month profit and fell -17.79%, which puts policy risk ahead of earnings in how the week was priced.
WIFI fell 7.91% in the week one holder sold its entire 26.67% and another took its stake past 50%
• Sinergi Graha Kapital exited its whole 26.67% holding on 19 November, and Investasi Sukses Bersama lifted its own from 27.28% to 54.42% the same day, which gives one holder majority control of WIFI.
• The 1.47 billion shares bought were about 23 times the 64.8 million WIFI traded that day, consistent with the stake changing hands outside the regular order book.
• Volume reached 309.2 million shares on Friday, about five times Thursday's, and the price still slipped -1.21% to IDR 815, so the heavy turnover found no follow-through bid.
The four big banks finished within 1% of flat while LQ45 fell 2.66%
• BBCA closed up +0.91%, BBNI up +0.85%, BBRI up +0.65% and BMRI unchanged, which made the banks the steadiest large weights in an index that lost ground every day from Tuesday.
• Bank Indonesia raised its policy rate 25 bps to 6% on 18 November and the day split the group, with BBCA up +4.55% while BBRI fell -1.94%, which says the market priced the hike bank by bank.
• The weakness reached beyond mining and banks, with ASII down -3.64% and ISAT down -8.00%, so the LQ45 decline touched conglomerates and telecoms as well as miners.

Summary
The bull read
• The big banks held their ground while the index fell, which says the heaviest weights in LQ45 still attract buyers when sentiment turns and gives the benchmark a floor the mining names did not supply.
• Bank Indonesia paired its rate hike with a lower reserve requirement, which leaves lenders such as BBRI and BMRI more room to keep funding loans at the higher policy rate.
• Profits kept growing at INDY and MDKA, so the mining sell-off sits on a stable earnings base and responds to a policy debate that is still open.
The bear read
• A record-low rupiah and an open door to another BI Rate rise in December keep funding costs for banks such as BBCA and BBRI pointing up, which keeps pressure on the index's heaviest sector.
• The state's claim on mining profits, a possible state equity stake and a single export channel remain proposals, so the risk premium on CUAN, INDY and MDKA stays until the terms are known.
• Mechanical selling is still ahead, with the MSCI exit of BRPT on 30 November and the FTSE Russell deletions on 21 December, so passive outflows can add to a market already down on the week.
What to watch next
Monitor price action and volume in BRPT into the 30 November close, when the MSCI changes take effect:
• If BRPT absorbs the index-fund selling with modest price damage, it supports the bull read that buyers remain behind the large caps.
• If BRPT sells off hard into the effective date and the weakness spreads to other LQ45 names, it adds weight to the bear read on mechanical outflows.
