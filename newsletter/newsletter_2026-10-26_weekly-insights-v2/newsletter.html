What the Data Unearthed
BBCA ended the week down 2.90% while BBRI, BMRI and BBNI gained 10% to 15%
• BBCA is the one large bank that did not recover: it fell 6.86% on Monday, rebounded 7.88% on Tuesday and still closed Friday at IDR 5,025, below the prior Friday's IDR 5,175.
• Foreign investors sold a net IDR 966.11B of BBCA on Tuesday, the largest single-name exit that day, while the stock rose, which says domestic buyers absorbed the foreign supply.
• The slide from Wednesday's IDR 5,275 close to Friday's IDR 5,025 came on lighter volume than Tuesday's 1.13 billion shares, so the fade lacked the heavy selling of the shock itself.
Seven of nine issuers that announced buybacks gained, in a week the LQ45 rose 7.57%
• Gains were widest in the Barito and Astra group names, with BREN up 28.30%, AALI up 23.00% and BRPT up 19.01%, while UNTR, BBRI and LSIP also beat the benchmark.
• CUAN fell 5.56% on 1.23 billion shares traded Tuesday with the price flat at IDR 505, and ICBP fell 5.16% after appearing among Tuesday's largest foreign net sells at IDR 107.3B, so an announcement did not lift every name.
• Eighteen of the LQ45's 45 constituents rose 10% or more, so the announcers' gains sit inside a rebound that reached well beyond them.
DMAS gained 34.59% and goes ex-dividend on 29 October
• The rise came with a sharp volume build: DMAS traded 992.06M shares on Wednesday, close to four times Tuesday's level, as the price moved from IDR 178 to IDR 208.
• The corporate action record shows an interim dividend of IDR 29 per share with a 29 October ex-date and a 13 November payment, equal to about 13.6% of Friday's IDR 214 close.
• Nine-month net profit rose 284% to IDR 1.49T on industrial land sales to the data center sector, and the interim dividend equals 93.75% of that profit.

Summary
The bull read
• The rebound reached nearly the whole LQ45 and the largest gains sat in state controlled banks and telecoms, which gives the recovery a local bid that does not wait for foreign flows to return.
• Issuer buybacks supply demand while the reserve requirement cut supplies bank liquidity, two domestic sources that stay active into November.
• BMRI and AKRA both reported double digit profit growth, which fits a selloff that priced governance risk while earnings kept growing.
The bear read
• Market capitalization closed the week well short of its level before the MSCI proposal, so most of the drawdown remains open.
• Foreign selling stayed concentrated in the largest banks even on the strongest rebound day, which leaves benchmark constituents like BBCA exposed to the MSCI outcome.
• A tighter auto-reject floor, IPO conduct sanctions and index exclusions reshape liquidity and ownership in the concentrated names, so price discovery there changes whatever MSCI decides.
What to watch next
Monitor BBCA and BBRI price action and volume into the 2 November change in JCI composition:
• If both banks hold their Friday levels on steady volume as the concentrated names leave the composite, it supports the bull read that domestic demand is carrying the benchmark.
• If heavy volume and renewed foreign net selling return to BBCA and BBRI around that date, it adds weight to the bear read that global funds remain the marginal price setters.
