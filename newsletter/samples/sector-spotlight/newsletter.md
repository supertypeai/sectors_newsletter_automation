# Banks Fell 9.4% in a Week and Now Trade at a Five-Year Low of 8.0x Earnings

Indonesian banks lost 9.4% of their combined market value in a week. After MSCI warned that Indonesia could be removed from its global indexes next year, the Jakarta Composite closed down 11.57% on 16 October and the finance index fell 8.98%.

The 48 listed banks are worth IDR 2,075.32T combined, are down **30.9%** this year, and now trade at a median **8.0x** earnings, the lowest of the five yearly readings available for the group. This issue explains what that number does and does not tell you, and which banks the sell-off actually hit.

## The Banks at a Glance

| Past week | Year to date | Median P/E |
|---|---|---|
| **-9.4%** combined market cap | **-30.9%** combined market cap | **8.0x** five-year low |

## How to Read the Numbers in This Issue

**P/E, the price of a year of profit.** A P/E of 8.0x means investors pay IDR 8 for every IDR 1 a bank earns in a year. A lower number means a lower price relative to profit, but a bank can also trade low because investors doubt that profit will last.

**Median and weighted, two ways to average.** The median is the bank in the middle, with half the banks on a lower P/E and half on a higher one. The weighted figure counts each bank by market cap, so the largest banks pull it hardest. When the two sit far apart, the group is not behaving as one group.

**P/B, the price of net worth.** P/B compares a share price with the accounting value of what the bank owns after its debts. Below 1x means the market values the bank at less than its books say.

## The Median Bank P/E Is Nearly Half Its 2025 Level

From 2022 to 2025 the median bank traded between 14.7x and 16.9x earnings. It now trades at 8.0x, about 46% below 2025. The median P/B has moved the same way, to 0.73x from a 0.81x to 0.98x range, so the market now prices the median bank below the accounting value of its net assets.

| Year | Median P/E |
|---|---|
| 2022 | 15.1x |
| 2023 | 16.9x |
| 2024 | 16.2x |
| 2025 | 14.7x |
| **2026** | **8.0x** |

*Banks sub-sector historical valuation. 2026 is the current-year reading as of 21 Oct 2026.*

**Median versus weighted**

The market-cap-weighted P/E is **16.1x**, twice the median. A gap that wide says one average hides two different groups: a crowd of low-multiple banks sets the median, while a few high-multiple ones lift the weighted figure. The four largest banks by market cap trade between 4.9x and 11.1x trailing earnings, below the weighted figure, so the lift comes from smaller lenders on high multiples, among them Bank Permata ([**BNLI**](https://sectors.app/idx/bnli?utm_source=newsletter&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=sector-spotlight_2026-10-22&utm_content=history&utm_term=bnli)) at 17.3x, Bank Jago ([**ARTO**](https://sectors.app/idx/arto?utm_source=newsletter&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=sector-spotlight_2026-10-22&utm_content=history&utm_term=arto)) at 24.3x, Super Bank Indonesia ([**SUPA**](https://sectors.app/idx/supa?utm_source=newsletter&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=sector-spotlight_2026-10-22&utm_content=history&utm_term=supa)) at 64.7x and Krom Bank Indonesia ([**BBSI**](https://sectors.app/idx/bbsi?utm_source=newsletter&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=sector-spotlight_2026-10-22&utm_content=history&utm_term=bbsi)) at 68.2x.

## Forecasts Assume Earnings Recover From a 19.4% Drop

A low P/E is only half the picture, because the "E" can change. The 8.0x median sits on earnings that shrank 19.4% in 2025. Forecasts for the sub-sector show them growing again.

| | 2025 realized | 2026 forecast | 2027 forecast |
|---|---|---|---|
| Earnings growth | **-19.4%** | **+16.5%** | **+25.3%** |
| Revenue growth | +1.3% | +20.1% | +28.9% |

*Market-cap-weighted growth for the banks sub-sector, realized 2025 and forecasts for 2026 and 2027.*

If 2026 earnings grew 16.5% with prices unchanged, the multiple on the larger earnings base would be lower than it is today. The forecasts are listed as published and may not yet reflect the past week's repricing, so treat them as the market's expectation before the sell-off, not after it.

## Low-Multiple Banks Fell Less, and the Median Describes Only Some of Them

> **In plain terms.** Indonesian banks are priced at half their 2025 multiple after a week in which foreign index access became a risk. The lowest-multiple banks are mostly state-owned and regional lenders, and they fell least. The highest-multiple small and digital lenders fell most.

**If you follow the economy.** A 9.4% weekly fall in the combined value of 48 banks reads as a statement about confidence in Indonesian markets more than about any one lender. The trigger was an index decision outside the banks' control, while forecasts still show their earnings growing.

**If you invest.** The 8.0x median is not a price any typical bank trades at: it describes a cluster of state and regional lenders, and the 16.1x weighted figure reflects a few high-multiple names. [**BNGA**](https://sectors.app/idx/bnga?utm_source=newsletter&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=sector-spotlight_2026-10-22&utm_content=takeaway&utm_term=bnga) shows the usual trade-off, the highest safety buffer and steady institutional buying, with a profit return that has slipped for a year and a 6.3x multiple that is not the lowest in the group.

> **What to watch.** Whether the forecast earnings recovery arrives while MSCI's possible reclassification of Indonesia stays unresolved through next year.
