Banks Fell 9.4% in a Week and Now Trade at a Five-Year Low of 8.0x Earnings

Indonesian banks lost 9.4% of their combined market value in a week. After MSCI warned that Indonesia could be removed from its global indexes next year, the Jakarta Composite closed down 11.57% on 16 October and the finance index fell 8.98%.

The 48 listed banks are worth IDR 2,075.32T combined, are down 30.9% this year, and now trade at a median 8.0x earnings, the lowest of the five yearly readings available for the group. This issue explains what that number does and does not tell you, and which banks the sell-off actually hit.

How to Read the Numbers in This Issue
P/E, the price of a year of profit

A P/E of 8.0x means investors pay IDR 8 for every IDR 1 a bank earns in a year. A lower number means a lower price relative to profit, but a bank can also trade low because investors doubt that profit will last.

Median and weighted, two ways to average

The median is the bank in the middle, with half the banks on a lower P/E and half on a higher one. The weighted figure counts each bank by market cap, so the largest banks pull it hardest. When the two sit far apart, the group is not behaving as one group.

P/B, the price of net worth

P/B compares a share price with the accounting value of what the bank owns after its debts. Below 1x means the market values the bank at less than its books say.

Valuation history
The Median Bank P/E Is Nearly Half Its 2025 Level

From 2022 to 2025 the median bank traded between 14.7x and 16.9x earnings. It now trades at 8.0x, about 46% below 2025. The median P/B has moved the same way, to 0.73x from a 0.81x to 0.98x range, so the market now prices the median bank below the accounting value of its net assets.

Median versus weighted

The market-cap-weighted P/E is 16.1x, twice the median. A gap that wide says one average hides two different groups: a crowd of low-multiple banks sets the median, while a few high-multiple ones lift the weighted figure. The four largest banks by market cap trade between 4.9x and 11.1x trailing earnings, below the weighted figure, so the lift comes from smaller lenders on high multiples, among them Bank Permata (BNLI) at 17.3x, Bank Jago (ARTO) at 24.3x, Super Bank Indonesia (SUPA) at 64.7x and Krom Bank Indonesia (BBSI) at 68.2x.

The Sell-Off Hit the Highest-Multiple Banks Hardest

The highest-multiple card shows four of the seven highest P/Es; Krom Bank Indonesia and Bank Sinarmas had no move in the data pulled, and Bank Mestika Dharma (27.6x) rose 2.5%.

The five lowest-multiple state-owned and regional banks lost 2.0% to 9.6%. The four high-multiple names with price data lost 17.2% to 23.7%. Bank Central Asia (BBCA), the largest bank at IDR 643.77T, sits between the two groups at 11.1x and fell 13.9%, more than the sub-sector's 9.4%. Only BNGA and BBMD rose. In plain terms, the heaviest losses landed on banks that had cost the most per rupiah of profit, while low-multiple banks held up better without escaping the fall.

The bank that held up
CIMB Niaga Has the Biggest Capital Buffer of Four, but Not the Lowest P/E or the Best ROE

Bank CIMB Niaga (BNGA) closed at IDR 1,725 on 21 October, up 2.7% on the week, after touching a 52-week low of IDR 1,435 on 19 October. Set against Bank Mandiri (BMRI), Bank Negara Indonesia (BBNI) and Bank Rakyat Indonesia (BBRI), it offers a trade-off rather than a clear bargain.

BNGA has the highest capital adequacy ratio of the four, 24.9%, and institutions bought it in every one of the last seven months while selling the other three in six or seven. The price of that comfort is profitability: its ROE of 11.8% is the second lowest, and BMRI earns 17.2% while trading at a lower P/E of 4.9x. Every bank here earned a lower ROE in 2025 than in 2024: BNGA from 13.0%, BMRI from 17.8%, BBNI from 12.8% and BBRI from 19.0%.

Earnings outlook

If 2026 earnings grew 16.5% with prices unchanged, the multiple on the larger earnings base would be lower than it is today. The forecasts are listed as published and may not yet reflect the past week's repricing, so treat them as the market's expectation before the sell-off, not after it.

Key takeaway
Low-Multiple Banks Fell Less, and the Median Describes Only Some of Them
In plain terms
Indonesian banks are priced at half their 2025 multiple after a week in which foreign index access became a risk. The lowest-multiple banks are mostly state-owned and regional lenders, and they fell least. The highest-multiple small and digital lenders fell most.
If you follow the economy

A 9.4% weekly fall in the combined value of 48 banks reads as a statement about confidence in Indonesian markets more than about any one lender. The trigger was an index decision outside the banks' control, while forecasts still show their earnings growing.

If you invest

The 8.0x median is not a price any typical bank trades at: it describes a cluster of state and regional lenders, and the 16.1x weighted figure reflects a few high-multiple names. BNGA shows the usual trade-off, the highest safety buffer and steady institutional buying, with a profit return that has slipped for a year and a 6.3x multiple that is not the lowest in the group.

What to watch
Whether the forecast earnings recovery arrives while MSCI's possible reclassification of Indonesia stays unresolved through next year.
