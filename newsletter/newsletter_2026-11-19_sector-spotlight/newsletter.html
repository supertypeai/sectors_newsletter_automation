Oil, Gas and Coal Split in Two: Coal Producers Edged Up 1.7% in a Week While Oil and Gas Names Fell 10.5%

Coal prices turned up this week. Newcastle thermal coal futures closed on Friday 13 November at USD 146.75 a ton, up 4.00% on the week, as Indian power plants restocked depleted inventories. The rebound recovered only part of the prior week's 7.45% drop, and Chinese demand stayed selective with stocks at four northern ports up 3.30% in a week to 26.41 million tons.

Energy prices hit the Oil, Gas & Coal sub-sector first. Its 88 listed companies hold IDR 1,144.95T in combined market cap, spanning coal miners, oil and gas producers, gas distributors, and offshore support services. The sector is down 53.2% year to date, trading at a median multiple of 9.3x earnings. But individual names moved in opposite directions this week.

How to Read the Numbers
P/E, the price of a year of profit

A P/E of 8.6x means investors pay IDR 8.6 for every IDR 1 of annual earnings. A low multiple means a cheap price relative to earnings, though it can also signal market doubts about whether profit will persist.

Median and weighted averages

The median company sits in the middle: half the sector trades on a lower P/E and half on a higher one. A market-cap-weighted average gives larger companies more weight. When the two numbers diverge, the sector is not moving as one.

ROE and net debt

ROE shows annual earnings generated per IDR 100 of equity, so 15.8% equals IDR 15.8 in profit. Net debt to EBITDA calculates how many years of operating profit clear debt after using cash. A negative figure means cash exceeds debt.

The Median P/E Is Back to Its 2023 and 2024 Level After a 2025 Spike

From 2022 to 2024 the median company traded between 4.9x and 9.0x earnings. In 2025 it reached 15.4x, and it now sits at 9.3x, about 39% below that peak and within half a turn of 2023 and 2024.

Alamtri Minerals: Net Debt Standout Among Coal Peers

Alamtri Minerals Indonesia (ADMR) closed at IDR 1,190 on 18 November, down 7.4% on the week, after touching a 52-week low of IDR 1,080 on 20 October. It is a coal producer, and Alamtri Resources Indonesia (ADRO) holds 85.4% of its shares. ADRO rose 2.2% over the same week. Against ADRO, Adaro Andalan Indonesia (AADI) and Indo Tambangraya Megah (ITMG), it stands out for one reason.

Key takeaway
A Split Sub-Sector
In plain terms
Coal and oil and gas names share a sub-sector but diverged this week. Coal producers held firm on rebounding commodity prices, while oil and gas stocks lost roughly a tenth of their value.

If you follow the economy. A four percent weekly move in Newcastle coal directly hits miner revenues. Export volumes remain capped by domestic market obligations and production quotas, ahead of a mandatory export portal launch scheduled for 1 January 2027.

If you invest. The 9.3x median reflects coal producers, while the 26.0x weighted average is skewed by two mega-caps near 25x and extreme outliers. Within coal, ADMR carries a premium multiple alongside net debt and fell while peers rose. Across all producers, 2026 P/E ratios reflect earnings that shrank by a third or more in 2025.

What to watch
Whether Newcastle coal stays above USD 140 through winter restocking while Indonesian export quotas restrict supply, and if miner earnings bounce back from 2025 lows.
