# Oil, Gas and Coal Split in Two: Coal Producers Edged Up 1.7% in a Week While Oil and Gas Names Fell 10.5%

Coal prices turned up this week. Newcastle thermal coal futures closed on Friday 13 November at USD 146.75 a ton, up 4.00% on the week, as Indian power plants restocked depleted inventories. The rebound recovered only part of the prior week's 7.45% drop, and Chinese demand stayed selective with stocks at four northern ports up 3.30% in a week to 26.41 million tons.

That matters because the Oil, Gas & Coal sub-sector is the part of the market where energy prices show up first. It holds 88 listed companies worth IDR 1,144.95T combined, covering coal miners, oil and gas producers, gas distributors and the shipping and service firms that work for them. The group is down **53.2%** year to date. This issue explains what its median multiple of **9.3x** earnings means, then shows that the week did not move the group as one.

## How to Read the Numbers in This Issue

**P/E, the price of a year of profit.** A P/E of 8.6x means investors pay IDR 8.6 for every IDR 1 a company earns in a year. A lower number means a lower price relative to profit, but a company can also trade low because investors doubt that profit will last.

**Median and weighted, two ways to average.** The median is the company in the middle, with half the group on a lower P/E and half on a higher one. The weighted figure counts each company by market cap, so the largest names pull it hardest. When the two sit far apart, the group is not behaving as one group.

**ROE and net debt, profit earned and debt carried.** ROE is the profit a company earns for every IDR 100 of shareholder equity, so 15.8% means IDR 15.8. Net debt to EBITDA counts the years of operating profit needed to repay debt after cash. A negative reading means the company holds more cash than debt.

From 2022 to 2024 the median company traded between 4.9x and 9.0x earnings. In 2025 it reached 15.4x, and it now sits at 9.3x, about 39% below that peak and within half a turn of 2023 and 2024.

**Median versus weighted**

The market-cap-weighted P/E is **26.0x**, about 2.8 times the median. A gap that wide says one average hides two groups: many companies at single-digit multiples set the median, while the largest names count for more in the weighted figure. ## One Sub-Sector, Two Different Weeks, and a Median That Describes Only the Coal Producers

> **In plain terms.** Coal and oil and gas companies share one sub-sector but did not share the week. Coal producers held steady as coal prices bounced, and the oil and gas names lost about a tenth of their value.

**If you follow the economy.** A four percent weekly move in the Newcastle benchmark matters to the listed producers because coal is what they sell. Indonesian export volumes remain limited by production quotas and domestic sales obligations, and a new export documentation portal is targeted for full operation on 1 January 2027.

**If you invest.** The 9.3x median describes a cluster of coal producers. The 26.0x weighted figure leans toward two very large names near 25x and a tail of very high multiples. Inside coal, [**ADMR**](https://sectors.app/idx/admr?utm_source=newsletter&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=sector-spotlight_2026-11-19&utm_content=takeaway&utm_term=admr) shows a trade-off between a higher multiple, the only net debt of its peer group and a weekly fall that its peers did not share. The 2026 P/E for every producer sits on profit that dropped by a third or more in 2025.

> **What to watch.** Whether Newcastle coal holds above USD 140 into the winter buying season while Indonesian export quotas stay tight, and whether profits at the four producers recover from their 2025 drop.
