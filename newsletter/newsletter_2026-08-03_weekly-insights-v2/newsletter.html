What the Data Unearthed
Metals & Minerals split down the middle, and so did foreign money
• Three Basic Materials names topped this week's LQ45 gainers: Indah Kiat Pulp & Paper ($INKP) +9.45% to IDR 8,400, Vale Indonesia ($INCO) +8.32% to IDR 5,275, and Barito Pacific ($BRPT) +5.73% to IDR 1,845, three different industries under one sector label.
• The same sector also produced one of the week's worst LQ45 performers: Aneka Tambang ($ANTM) -3.68% to IDR 2,880. $INCO and $ANTM sit in the identical Metals & Minerals industry classification.
• Foreign flow moved with price in both names: $INCO drew a net IDR 80.20B foreign buy this week, while $ANTM saw a net IDR 202.99B foreign sell, the largest of any name in this issue (exchange definition, idx_daily_data).
• $INCO's Q2 2026 net profit rose +313% year on year on higher nickel prices, in results reported this week. (Kontan) $BRPT's own first-half 2026 net profit reached US$518 million on US$5.68 billion in revenue, also reported this week. (Investor.id)
• A sector-level read would have called Basic Materials broadly positive this week and said nothing about the split running straight through its own Metals & Minerals names.
Outside the LQ45 table, Timah's profit tripled and foreign money followed
• PT Timah ($TINS) is not an LQ45 constituent, so it never appears in the tables above, but it closed the week +10.79%, from IDR 3,430 to IDR 3,800, with Friday's volume of 135.49M shares its heaviest session of the week by a wide margin.
• It was also the second-biggest net foreign buy of any name in the entire market this week, IDR 246.64B, behind only $BBCA's IDR 431.22B (exchange definition, idx_daily_data).
• Timah's Q2 2026 net profit grew +304.56% year on year, with revenue up +17.41%, per its own quarterly financials (sectors.app).
• The three biggest net foreign buys of the week, in order, were $BBCA, $TINS and $BRPT, two of which also posted a double-digit weekly price move.
A five-month insider seller finished exiting IATA, and the stock jumped 33% two sessions later
• MNC Asia Holding filed on 30 July to disclose it had sold its remaining 1,795,776,366 shares of MNC Energy Investments ($IATA), taking its stake from 5.74% to 0.0%. The shares sold at an average of IDR 59.995 across two dated trades, 21 and 28 July, for a combined IDR 107.74B.
• It is the seventh such disposal in five months: IDR 232.30B sold in total across 3.63B shares at an average of IDR 64 per share, per the filing history shown above.
• $IATA closed at IDR 57 on the day the exit was filed. Two sessions later, on 31 July, it closed at IDR 76, up +33.33% in a single day, on volume of 1.64B shares, its heaviest session of the week and enough to place it third on Friday's most-traded list.
• The filing and the rally are two separate, dated facts. Nothing in either confirms one caused the other.

The Other Side
The bull read
• The index closed the week at its own high, IDR 10,919.63T on Friday, after a Wednesday trough of IDR 10,671.93T, a genuine within-week recovery rather than a single-day pop.
• LQ45 (+1.05%) and IDX30 (+1.09%) both outran the whole market's +0.48%, and large-cap breadth was solidly positive at 29 up against 11 down.
• Two of the week's five biggest LQ45 gainers, $INCO and $BRPT, backed their price moves with real foreign buying and reported earnings growth, and $TINS, outside the LQ45 table entirely, backed a +10.79% week with the market's second-largest net foreign buy.
The bear read
• Foreign investors were net sellers market-wide of IDR 1.34T this week even as the index rose, meaning domestic buying, not foreign inflows, carried the tape.
• $ANTM, in the identical Metals & Minerals industry as gainer $INCO, fell -3.68% against the largest net foreign sell of any name in this issue, IDR 202.99B out, and $UNTR closed the week as the worst LQ45 performer at -6.08%.
• Bank Indonesia's governor resigned two years early on 27 July, and S&P Global Ratings has flagged the transition as a source of monetary-policy uncertainty ahead of the 18-19 August rate decision, precisely when $BBCA, this week's single biggest net foreign buy, and the banking sector have the most riding on the outcome.

What would settle it: BI's 18-19 August RDG is the first real test of whether the post-Perry-Warjiyo transition unsettles the rate path investors have been pricing in. A steady policy hold, alongside a benign July CPI print due the morning this issue goes out, would support the bull case; a surprise move or a hawkish tone from the RDG would support the bear case.
